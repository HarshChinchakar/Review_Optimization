## 3. Who is actually reading these reviews

### Primary audience — the resident **[inferred, high confidence]**
Women aged roughly 21–30, working in the Hinjewadi / Wakad IT and BPO belt or studying nearby. Kalewadi sits
on the Pimpri-Chinchwad side of the Wakad bridge — a 10–20 minute commute to Rajiv Gandhi Infotech Park, which
is the entire reason this micro-market exists. She is usually new to Pune, often relocating for a first or
second job, deciding in a hurry, from a phone, comparing 3–5 options she found on Google Maps, 99acres,
NoBroker or JustDial.

What she is scared of, in her own order of priority:
1. **Is it safe, and who else lives there?** (gate timings, CCTV, warden, male entry policy, locality after 9pm)
2. **Is the food actually edible?** — the number one reason PG residents leave.
3. **Will the owner be decent to me?** (deposit returned, complaints acted on, not creepy, not arbitrary)
4. **How bad is the commute in Hinjewadi traffic?**
5. **What am I really paying?** (deposit, food charges, electricity extra, notice period)
6. Wi-Fi that survives a work-from-home call, hot water, water supply, power backup, laundry.

### Second audience — the parent **[inferred, high confidence]**
For a female-only PG in India this is a co-decision maker and frequently the *veto*. Parents read reviews
looking for exactly two words: **safe** and **homely**. A review that says "my parents were relaxed after
seeing the place" is worth more to this business than five reviews saying "good PG".

### Third audience — Google's ranking systems
The local pack ranks on relevance, distance and prominence. Review *text* feeds relevance: a profile whose
reviews repeatedly contain "PG for girls in Kalewadi", "near Hinjewadi", "homely food" is far more likely to
surface for those exact searches than a profile with 63 reviews that all say "nice place, good service".
**This is the entire commercial thesis of the product we are building.**

### Executive PG audience **[inferred]**
Working professionals, likely a mixed or male property, older skew (25–35), paying more, comparing against
co-living brands (Housr, Colive, Stanza). This buyer cares about privacy (single occupancy), Wi-Fi speed,
housekeeping frequency, and not having a curfew. Safety language is *not* the lead here — convenience and
"no-nonsense, professional" is. Its chips should sound different in tone from the female-only property's.

## 5. The psychology to design against

- **The resident is not a writer, and she knows it.** The blank Google review box asks her to perform. She
  abandons not because she is unwilling but because she cannot think of what to say that sounds adequate.
  Handing her true statements to *approve* removes the performance anxiety entirely. This is the core insight.
- **She is grateful, and gratitude decays fast.** The highest-intent moments are: move-in day (relief), the
  first time the owner fixes something (surprise), festival food, and the day she gets her deposit back.
  A permanent QR at the entrance catches the ambient case; the owner asking at those four moments is what
  actually fills the profile.
- **Safety is a topic she will not free-type about.** Writing "I feel safe here" in public feels like
  advertising your address. But *tapping a chip* that says "Safe for girls" is frictionless, and the AI
  turning it into a sentence launders that discomfort away. Chips beat text boxes specifically here.
- **Nobody wants to be the person who wrote the fake-sounding review.** If the generated text sounds like
  marketing, she will not post it, and she will think slightly less of you. The output must sound like her.
  This is a conversion requirement before it is an ethics requirement — the two happen to align.
- **The owner-relationship is the asset.** "Caring owner" already dominates your reviews. Chips should let
  people say it in the *specific* ways they mean it: responds quickly, treats residents like family, fair
  about the deposit, sorted a problem the same day.
- **Strict rules are a feature to this audience, if framed by a resident.** The business cannot say "we are
  strict" without sounding unpleasant. A resident saying "rules are strict but that is exactly why my parents
  were fine with it" converts the concern into the strongest possible trust signal. Ship a chip for it.

## 6. What this means for the build (carried into the app)

1. **Two chip sets, one deployment.** `?loc=female` and `?loc=executive`, one QR per property.
2. **Tier 1 keywords occupy the first row** of chips, phrased as a resident would say them, not as a
   search string. `Feels safe for girls` reads human; `safe PG for girls in Pune` reads like SEO. The chip
   carries the human phrasing; the *prompt* carries the search phrasing and lets the model bridge them.
3. **A "for my parents" chip exists on Location 1** and nowhere else.
4. **Food, Wi-Fi, hot water, power backup chips exist** because the profile is currently silent on all four.
5. **A "strict but fair rules" chip exists** — turning your only recurring criticism into a trust asset.
6. **Location 2's chip tone is professional, not protective.**
7. **Length varies** — 63 reviews averaging 4.8★ that all suddenly become identically-shaped paragraphs is a
   visible pattern on a small profile. Short reviews are the majority of any real profile.

## 7. Risks specific to this business

- **Small profile, sudden volume.** Going from ~63 reviews to a burst of new ones in a week is the pattern
  Google's spam systems look at hardest. Aim for a steady trickle — a handful a week, from real residents,
  from their own phones and their own networks. Do not run a "everyone review us today" drive.
- **One device, many reviews.** Never let residents post from a reception phone or the owner's phone. Same
  device fingerprint across reviews gets the whole cluster filtered. The per-IP limit in the app is the
  backstop; the operational rule matters more.
- **Never offer a rent discount for a positive review.** Rent concession for a *review* is already risky;
  for a *positive* review it is a clear violation and, on a 63-review profile, easy to spot in the pattern.
- **Ex-residents are your best untapped source** — someone who stayed a year and left happy writes the most
  credible review, and it is safe to ask for it after they leave.
